Step 0: the prospect intake form, before and after

The first surface a prospect ever touches, and the last one still running its own look after the rest of the funnel was rebuilt. Left is what shipped. Right is the new system. Same fixture as the rest of the review: Kwame, an outbound prospecting agent for Ambitt Media.

Every frame is the real React form driven end to end by a browser, with the AI question call stubbed so the tailored slides render the same way twice. Both columns were captured under identical conditions: the "before" column is served from a throwaway git worktree at the commit before this work, so the only difference between the two sides is this change. Back to the full funnel review.

If the frames below are blank, the screenshots are missing: docs/email-review/**/shots/ is gitignored on purpose, because the rendered pages are committed and the images are regenerable.

What was wrong

The previous pass fixed this page's failing colours and deliberately left everything else, on the grounds that it is a form rather than a document. The result was that step 0 stayed in a different world from steps 1 to 6.

  1. A different palette entirely. Warm neutral (#fafaf8 page, #171717 ink, #737373 helper text) against the funnel's slate. Nothing about the two surfaces looked related.
  2. A near-black sidebar carrying an 84px chapter numeral and a decorative pull-quote attributed to Atlas. It was the only dark surface anywhere in the funnel, it was the warm black rather than the system's, and it spent the entire left column on ornament while the prospect's real question, "how much more of this is there", went unanswered.
  3. The primary button on two steps was invisible. .fa-btn-primary and .fa-btn-secondary were used on the domain-check, question and review slides and were defined nowhere. The stylesheet's own :where(button) reset stripped them to bare text, so "Looks right, keep going" and "Send it" rendered as unstyled words on the two screens that carry the conversion.
  4. The first screen promised a brief that does not exist. "The 7-chapter brief", listing The job deeper, How it works, Hard limits and Tools. Those four chapters were removed when the flow became adaptive: after the one-sentence answer, Atlas writes the rest of the questions. A prospect was being shown a contents page for a document they would never be given, and the header said "Step 1 of 3" while the sidebar said "Chapter 01 / 07" on the same screen.
  5. Progress disappeared exactly where it was needed. The tailored questions rendered in a bare centred column with no rail and no step indicator, which is the longest and least predictable stretch of the form.
  6. Contrast failures the previous pass missed, listed in the table below. They were in inline styles on the adaptive slides and in the shared helper colours, not in the class names that pass audited.
  7. A gradient button with a teal glow, a 135deg gradient panel outlined in 1px #00b3b3, gray 1px card borders on the contents list, the review blocks and the timeline, an outline upload glyph, and #00d4d4 still alive in the Atlas visor.

Slide 1 · the first thing anyone sees

Welcome

The contents list now describes the four steps that actually happen, and says so in Atlas's voice. The heading drops from 44px to 30px at weight 500, so hierarchy comes from size and space rather than bulk. The gradient-and-glow button is a flat fill that clears AA. "Progress saved automatically" was not true for the tailored answers, which are only saved on send, so it now says what is true: best done in one sitting.

Two things worth your eye

  • The dark sidebar is gone. That is the biggest judgement call here and the easiest one to disagree with. The reasoning: it was the only dark surface in the whole pre-sale funnel, it was warm black against a cool system, and the same treatment was removed from the proposal in the previous pass for reading like a system notice rather than a letter. If you want the drama back, the rail is one token change, but it would put step 0 back on its own island.
  • The contents list now says four steps, not seven. This changes client-facing copy on the first screen, which was outside a pure styling brief. It was shipping a promise the product does not keep, so it seemed worse to leave it. The four steps match what the flow actually does.
