Agent emails, before and after

Same content in both columns: Arthur, a CRE sourcing agent, working a Dallas industrial shortlist for Litsey Creek Partners. Left is what we send today. Right is the new system.

Scroll inside each panel. Every render is a real file produced by the real template, not a mockup.

Agent response

The one clients see most, and the one that has to carry the relationship. Before: serif headings that fight the portal, an avatar floating with no name attached, raw tool names ("google sheets append row") shown to the client. After: a letterhead that says who this is from, the numbers as an editorial strip instead of boxed tiles, the systems we touched named the way the client names them, and the portal offered rather than assigned.

Welcome

First impression, and the worst offender before: a black letter-avatar, green tool pills, an amber box with a coloured 1px border, a blue link. Four unrelated accent colours and no teal anywhere. After: the agent has a face, the brief sits in a tonal panel with no border, tools are teal chips, and the promise about access is stated in words.

Approval request

The highest-stakes email we send: something is about to happen under the client's name. Before: a brown-black warning slab, "WHAT I'LL DO" and "WHY" in all caps, a black "Approve This Action" button. After: an amber rule and one chip carry the state, the consequence gets its own panel and a plain sentence, and the button says "Yes, go ahead". The reassurance line at the bottom is new.

Weekly digest

Before: a near-black header slab, five all-caps labels, grey stat tiles, a black "View Full Report" button. It read like a monitoring tool. After: the summary leads, the numbers sit between two hairlines, statuses keep their meaning in a colour that survives dark mode, and there is exactly one thing to decide.
