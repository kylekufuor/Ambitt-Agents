What was actually wrong

Our post-sale emails were rebuilt on one design system. The pre-sale funnel was never touched, so the documents a prospect decides money on were running four unrelated looks, and two accessibility failures were shipping inside them.

  1. A webfont that was never loaded. The proposal declared Geist over a CDN; the quote and all three emails just named 'Inter' with nothing behind it. Gmail drops the first and has never had the second, so the typeface a prospect actually saw was whatever their client picked. Both now use the same system stack as every other Ambitt email, so what we design is what they read.
  2. A second teal nobody chose. #00d4d4 ran alongside the brand #00b3b3 in the proposal and the intake form. It is gone.
  3. Teal used as text and as a button fill. #00b3b3 is 2.59:1 on white. It failed WCAG AA as link text, as a label on a button, and as a focus ring. Now it is a mark only, and text and buttons use the deep steps.
  4. Six stacked cards with 1px gray borders in the quote, which is the single loudest generic-AI tell and is banned in client-portal/DESIGN.md. Surfaces now separate by tone.
  5. Em dashes in hosted copy. Four client-facing strings on the portal status pages carried them. Those pages never pass through the send-time scrub, so they were shipping. Fixed at source, and the test now covers the quote.

0. The intake form

Step 0, and the last surface still running its own look. It was left alone in the first pass on the grounds that a form is not a document, which meant the very first thing a prospect touches stayed in a different palette from everything after it. It now runs the same system. Three things beyond the re-skin: the primary button on the domain-check and review steps was rendering as bare text, because the two classes it used were never defined anywhere; the first screen promised a "7-chapter brief" whose last four chapters no longer exist; and the tailored questions had no progress indicator at all. 3. The proposal

The biggest change. A full-bleed near-black slab ran across the top carrying cyan #00d4d4, which is the exact treatment we removed from the agent emails because it reads as a system notice rather than a letter. It is replaced by a document title block: mark, state chip, headline, and the specs as a proper spec sheet with tabular figures and hairline rows. That spec sheet is the one thing worth remembering here, and it says "this is the machine we are building for you" without a box around it. The sample email and the morning digest are now treated as quoted figures, because they depict a different surface. Nothing was cut: every field the data contract carries is still on the page.

What did not change

The data contracts. ProposalEmailData and QuoteData in types.ts, and the Zod schemas that mirror them, are untouched, so the Atlas prompts still emit exactly what they emitted before. Every Handlebars expression in both templates maps one to one onto the old ones, including the inline hooks Atlas writes into its own strings: span.accent, em, and the header-row types link / subject / text. The scope-item icons changed what they draw, not what they key off.

Update. The intake form at /onboard/[token] was left on its own layout in that pass, on the grounds that it is an interactive form rather than a document. It has since been brought onto the same system as step 0 of this review, above. Correcting its 37 failing colour pairs did not catch everything: the adaptive slides carried their failures in inline styles rather than in classes, so the domain-check panel labels (2.49:1), the scale buttons (2.59:1), the shared #a3a3a3 microcopy (2.52:1) and every control boundary (1.42:1) were still shipping. Those are fixed too.
