Proposal · client portal · 29 July 2026

The portal is a worklist, not a CRM.

Same objects Kyle's asking for (lead-shaped records, familiar layout, one surface that serves brokers, tax pros and home services at once) with one word changed. A CRM is a system of record the human maintains. A worklist is a report from someone who works for you, and the only thing it asks you to do is decide. Every token, icon and type rule below comes from client-portal/DESIGN.md. Full argument in RATIONALE.md.

Q1See firstNot a metric. The handoff: “Arthur drafted six emails and stopped.” Work done and supervision proven in one line. The $27 becomes a five-cell verb ledger further down the page.
Q2Queue, not a boardOne prioritised column on desktop, the identical column at 390px. The pipeline is the same list grouped by stage: a board rotated 90°, which is what makes it phone-native.
Q3AttributionEvery line starts with “Arthur”. There is a next run time. There is no Add button. And his reasoning is set as prose, in a second typographic register no CRM has.
Q4Approvals are homeThe root route is the approval queue, not a tab off it. Batch card first, per-draft review as an expansion, never six navigations. Decided items fall into the log so you see what your OK caused.
Q5Density vs restraintRows, not cells. A 12-column grid would need cell borders, 12px type and zebra stripes on day one. Casey has 15 records, not 4,000: she needs readable, not dense.

The mark, the accent stripe on a card that's waiting, the rule beside Arthur's own words, the ledger numerals, the one word in the headline, and the primary button. Never a status pill.

One rule I'd hold the line on. The accent stripe on the left edge of a card is decoration, so a card that's waiting on Casey also says “3 days waiting” in words, and a follow-up row also says “Day 7 today”. Colour is never the only signal, on any of these screens.
